As light travels across the surface, the face is no longer confined to the fixity of a portrait. In cultural heritage imaging, Reflectance Transformation Imaging is valued for its ability to reveal subtle surface details and traces that conventional photography may leave unseen. Yet its significance extends beyond documentation and analysis: RTI also possesses a striking artistic potential, transforming the act of looking into an encounter with light, surface and perception.

Lit from different angles, the same face can appear to shift, fragment and re-emerge. Textures, fractures and traces come forward and recede with every movement of the light, while the image itself becomes fluid rather than fixed. The portrait thus moves beyond the representation of a face; it becomes an evolving visual experience in which surface, light and memory continuously reshape one another. What is revealed is not only what the face contains, but what the changing light makes possible to see, perceive and imagine.
